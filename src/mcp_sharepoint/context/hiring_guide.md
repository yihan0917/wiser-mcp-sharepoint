# Hiring Guide

## Overview

Here we keep all the information related to our hiring practices and procedures. This includes how we source candidates, lists of job reqs, interview guidelines, what we look for in candidates, example questions, etc. The goal of this hiring guide is to promote consistency, alignment and efficiency in our process.

---

## Planning for New Roles

### Annual Budgeting

We typically propose budget for new positions on the team during Q4 for the annual budgeting/planning process. These positions are based on needs identified by the team, typically taking into account: skill gaps, single points of failure, and inability to keep up with business demand for our services. The goals for the coming year may also drive hiring if we need additional staff to accomplish corporate or team goals. Given we have a corporate goal of reaching profitability by 2026, we will continue to be conservative when approving additional roles.

Managers need to identify new needs to their senior engineering leader by September 1st. All requests with justifications need to be reviewed with the CTO by October 1st to be considered during the budget review. The CTO will work with the recruiting team and the senior engineering leadership team to identify the location and market compensation for each proposed new role.

The proposals will be evaluated by the executive team during the budget review. New hires for the coming year will be approved by the CEO and CFO after CTO presentation during the budget review. Approved hires will be communicated back to the teams by the end of December. 

### In-Year Requests

During the year, we may identify a need for a new position that should be hired before the next budgeting cycle. Causes include unexpected customer work, executive decision to increase priority of certain roadmap items, attrition, or top grading of underperforming team members.

The CTO is responsible for assessing hiring needs with each of the senior engineering leaders, and working with the executive team to get budget approval. Outside of exceptional circumstances (these are rare) budget increases require an offsetting budget reduction. These offsets will usually come from within the portfolio making the request, though sometimes the portfolio team will get agreement from another portfolio within Engineering or from another department to share budget to offset the cost.

Whether a role is requested during the budget cycle or during the year, it must be approved by the CEO and CFO (via CTO request) and added to the budget before the role can be opened for recruiting.

Opening a New Position
A role can be opened once the role has been planned and approved. 

Job Description
The hiring manager must write the job description for the role and provide it to their senior engineering leader. The job description must be clear in identifying the job title, the expectations for the role, the skills and experience required, as well as what the new hire in the role will be working on.  The goal of the job description is to be eye-catching to potential candidates, to give just enough information to describe the need as well as to generate interest/excitement.

Hiring Plan
In addition to a job description, the hiring manager must create the hiring plan. The hiring plan specifies the set of interviewers for the role, which stage in the process each interviewer will participate in, and what topics/areas each interviewer will focus on. The recruiting team will load this into the applicant tracking system so that each interviewer will be prompted by the system post-interview to provide feedback on those specific areas.

Scorecard (Optional)
At one point we required the hiring manager create a scorecard for the role, but have moved away from that in recent years. The competencies contained within the scorecard have largely been moved to the hiring plan and the near term objectives have become part of the onboarding plan. One downside of this change is that the near term objectives would be useful input to the interviewers to evaluate a candidate’s ability to be successful in the role. This is a recommended but not required step in the process.

Sourcing Candidates
Our internal recruiting team is primarily responsible for sourcing candidates. They will use a mixture of job postings on career sites and active candidate outreach to fill the hiring pipeline. It is up to the hiring manager to ensure their recruiting partner understands the requirements of the role so they puts strong, relevant candidates in front of the hiring manager for consideration.

Given our current internal recruiter capacity, we are not engaging with external staffing partners to fill roles. The CTO typically sets aside a small amount of budget each year should we have a particularly difficult time filling a position. The hiring manager may request the use of an external staffing partner if an extended period of time has passed without filling a role. The CTO will consider the request based on the situation and available budget. The internal recruiting team will only engage with a staffing partner upon CTO approval.

Referral Bonuses
Our first preference for hiring new team members is always through the referrals of existing team members.  Our current team knows best what it is like to work here and what it takes to be successful.  We typically offer an incentive/referral bonus with critical new Engineering openings. These referral bonus policies and amounts vary by country. The latest can be found here on SharePoint. Typically, referral hires must be employed for 90 days before the bonus is paid. Also, not all employees are eligible. See the specific policies for more information.

Resume Review
The hiring manager is responsible to review & filter incoming applications for their open positions. The recruiting partner will filter out applications that clearly do not fit the requirements for the role and pass on the remainder to the hiring manager to review. We expect our hiring managers to review resumes with 2 business days of submission. The hiring manager should tell their recruiting partner which applications should be selected for screening and which should be rejected. For rejections, if there is an obvious reason for rejection that the hiring manager would like to be added to the filter criteria, they should indicate that in their rejection message back to their recruiting partner.

Screening Candidates
Initial Screen - Recruiting Team
Typically the first point of contact for a candidate will be the recruiting partner. The recruiter will screen candidates for communication skills, interest in the role, and salary expectation. They will get any information that may have not been provided by the candidate in their application such as location, contact information or inconsistencies in their resume. The recruiter can also ask a set of screening questions if provided by the hiring manager as part of the hiring plan. These screening questions can give the hiring manager additional information that will help them determine if the candidate should continue in the process. 

Initial Screen - Hiring Manager
After the recruiter screen, the hiring manager reviews their screen notes and decides if they should speak with the candidate. Given the time investment for a panel interview round, it is important to quickly screen out the candidates that will not fit.  The manager phone screen is a 30-60 minute session where the hiring manager focuses on the candidate’s fit and likelihood to succeed in the interview process.  

The goal with each question is to get the candidate talking about their experiences with good examples.  Ask questions starting with "What" / "How" / "Tell Me More" to get the candidate talking.  Use the details of their stories to get a preliminary read on technical ability.  The focus of the screen is to knock out culture fit issues, people with no ambition or goals, people that show no self awareness or insight from past experiences, etc.

Why no technical questions in this screen?  We will validate tech skills (knowledge and aptitude) during the next screen and in focused panel interviews.

The bar for passing this screen is finding the candidate who has career interests that align with the role we are looking to fill, is really self-aware about their strengths and weaknesses, and exhibits no glaring red flags of being a fit with our culture.

Technical Phone Screen - Hiring Manager or Team Member
If the candidate passes the sniff test from the initial hiring manager phone screen, we typically do a 2nd 45 minute phone screen that is more technical in nature.  This screen is broken down into 2 parts: technical questions and a coding question.  Reserve 5 minutes at the end for any additional questions the candidate may have.

Technical Questions
Spend the first 20 minutes digging into the specialty area of the candidate.  For exampel if the candidate is a front-end engineer, assess their knowledge of CSS and JavaScript, as well as their knowledge of good front-end coding practices and tools.

Coding Question
Spend the next 20 minutes doing a coding exercise.  For this exercise you should use Coderpad so you can see what the candidate is typing and collaborate directly with them.  We have a paid Coderpad account and HR should include a link in the meeting invite. Give them a reasonable problem that can be solved in a few lines of code, ask them to test it, ask them to make it better (if time allows).

We're not looking for perfection here, but we're looking for evidence in this interview session that the person writes code for a living and wouldn't completely bomb during an in-person round of interviews.  Bringing someone into the office is an expensive investment of the team's time (3-4 person hours for the interviews plus another 30 minutes for the whole team for a recap), so we want to knock poor candidates out of the process as early as possible.

Panel Round
For most engineering candidates, we will complete the interview process with a single panel round.  For more senior candidates it may be necessary to bring back the person to meet additional people (particularly if that candidate is going to have direct reports who they may not meet all of them during the initial round).

The panel round interviews will generally consist of a few different types of sessions:

Focused Technical Interview - these are intended to validate the different technical skill areas required for success in the role.  They generally break down as follows:

Coding Interview - Whiteboard some coding problems with a team member.  If you write code for a living, we want to make sure you are good at it.  

Relatively straightfoward problems - requires no more than 10 lines of code to solve

Exercises the candidate's ability to think without requiring knowledge of specific tricks or domains

Assess the candidate's ability to describe the complexity/runtime of an algorithm, discuss tradeoffs of data structure choices, testability, etc.

Design Interview - Ask the candidate to design something that exercises modeling skills

Assess the candidate's ability to correctly model relationships, assign responsibilities, etc.

Assess the candidate's ability to structure a problem, ask clarifying questions, refactor if they go down the wrong path, etc.

Domain Expertise Interview - Ask the candidates specifics about their domain.  Mobile developers should understand mobile development.  Database developers must understand databases.  Managers must understand how to build and lead great teams.

Focused Behavioral Interview - these interviews are intended to validate soft skills, which are just as important to our success as hard technical skills.  We ask for specific examples that demonstrate the competencies required for the candidate to be a successful culture fit and to have alignment with our culture principles.  See 
Culture Questions
 for examples.

For each candidate that interviews for a given position, it is best to keep the same interviewers and same set of questions.  This will allow for an accurate cross-candidate comparison.  See Interview Guidelines for more details and Interview Questions for examples of standard questions we ask candidates.

Post-Interview Feedback
After the completion of each interview, all interviewers must write up their notes and fill out the ratings for their assigned areas in the applicant tracking system.  Each interviewer must provide backing detail as to why they rated the candidate a certain way for each area.  If the interviewer cannot provide this detail, they did not adequately perform their assessment. The hiring manager is expected to review the panel round feedback within 2 business days and either make a hiring decision or to have the recruiter schedule a review feedback meeting to further the candidate.

Reference Checks
We always check a minimum of 2-3 references for a candidate.  These references are the ones chosen by the hiring manager based on interview conversations (not necessarily those given to us by the candidate).  Ask the candidate to help set up the interview.  Past bosses, peers, subordinates.

As part of the reference check we also do a formal background check (HR/Office Manager handles this task).

Any serious concerns uncovered during the reference check process should be weighed carefully into the hiring decision.  We do not extend a written offer until the reference checks are complete, though we may extend a verbal offer contingent upon successful reference checks.

Process Timeliness
Speed wins in recruiting. Staying engaged with candidates throughout the process and moving them through it quickly shows that we are interested in them and respectful of their time. Our goal is that we move all candidates through the process within 2 weeks from the time they apply. This includes making timely decisions on final round candidates. If we have a couple of candidates in the final round at the same time, it is ok to keep everyone on hold for a few days until all have finished the process. The recruiter will clearly indicate this to each candidate. It is not ok to keep a candidate on hold indefinitely to see if we can find someone better. If there isn’t an immediately better candidate we should reject and move on. If there are two well qualified candidates for a role, it is ok to keep one candidate on hold for a few days to allow the preferred candidate to decide whether or not to accept an offer. Please keep the candidate’s experience in mind when making such decisions.

Offer
A verbal offer is always extended to the candidate by the HR team via phone or video call.  This verbal offer must be approved by HR, CEO, and CTO before it can be presented to the candidate.  Our current HR process is that we get a verbal acceptance from the candidate before providing the formal written offer.

The recruiting team will make a recommendation on compensation based on a market analysis.  The offer must be within the budget for the role unless the CTO has an approved exception. We always put forth our best offer first, taking into account the budget, the market value for the position, and the salary requirements of the candidate. Compensation packages for engineers will consist of base salary and sometimes bonus and/or equity compensation. Bonus programs are not available in all countries - in these countries the bonus compensation is instead included in the base salary. Equity packages are currently only available in the US, though we are evaluating a similar program for employees in other locations.

Post-Offer / Pre-Start
The hiring manager should stay engaged with the accepted candidate during the period between the offer acceptance and start date. Have relevant team members (or executives) send welcome messages. Be available to answer questions the candidate may have before starting. We sometimes lose candidates to other companies in between offer acceptance and start, especially in countries with long notice periods. In these countries we sometimes offer a small sign on bonus to incent the candidate to follow through on their acceptance and join our team.

Follow Up With Rejected Final Round Candidates
For any candidate that makes it through to the end of the process but does not get an offer, a personal thank you email from the hiring manager is recommended. We want all candidates to have a good experience, including those we do not choose to join our team. These candidates have invested a good amount of their time in our process, so the least we can do is send them a personal thank you for their time and wish them well in their job search. If the rejected candidates request feedback for why they are not chosen, it is ok to share feedback if it will help them in their job search. Examples of this would include sharing where they missed on a correct solution to a coding problem or answered tech questions incorrectly. It would be best to avoid subjective evaluation areas where the candidate did not score highly, as the candidate is unlikely to agree with your opinion.

## Interview Meeting Guidelines

For the comfort of the candidate, be sure to do the following:

Greet the candidate by name when you arrive

If you are not sure how to pronounce the candidate’s name, just ask them. This is preferable to not saying their name or butchering the pronunciation.

Tell the candidate the objective(s) you plan to cover during your interview session

Keep the candidate focused and on-point, don't be afraid to interrupt and steer them back on track

If the candidate gets stuck, allow a little bit of uncomfortable silence - don't give them the answer but don't let them hang for 10 minutes either

Always leave time at the end for questions

Don't answer a question you don't know the answer to or don't feel comfortable answering - direct them to ask that question of the hiring manager, senior engineering leader, or CTO

Don't forget to sell - we want to excite our candidates about working for Wiser (even if they clearly are not a fit)

## Applicant Tracking System
We keep track of each candidate that applies and the details around their experience: position applied/interviewed for, dates, interview team, feedback.

The tool we use to track applicants is SmartRecruiters. The recruiting team will invite all hiring managers and interviewers to this system. All team members will use it collect candidate feedback.
