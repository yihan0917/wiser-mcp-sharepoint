# Engineering Overview

## Summary

With Wiser's organic and acquisition growth, we have brought together many teams with different cultures. It's vital to our success to create a unified culture and strategy so that we can work effectively together to achieve our company mission and goals.

## Why This Matters

This is important for team members to understand so that we are always building toward the technical vision needed to achieve the company vision, we are focusing our efforts in the right areas, and we are all aligned on the same approach to building solutions. The latter is important so that teams can work independently but cohesively towards shared goals and that we can deliver the most value together per effort invested.

## How to Use This Information

Use this section to better understand our expectations for delivering solutions for Wiser and what you need to be thinking about as you design, build and maintain these solutions. Ask questions if the solution your team is working on does not align with our principles, fit our strategy, or you see behavior out of line with our values.

---

## Engineering's Why (Mission & Vision)
The Engineering mission statement supports and enables our company mission. We are here to be the first to build a commerce execution platform.

Building that platform and delivering the solutions on top of it for our customers needs to be done with high quality given the high level of trust our customers need to place on our solutions to power their business decisions. Our approach also needs to enable us to deliver value quickly and to build systems that can easily integrate acquired businesses (a key part of our growth strategy).

### Benefits of Evolutionary Architecture

- **Flexibility and Adaptability** - Systems can quickly adapt to new requirements, technologies, or changes in the business environment

- **Reduced Risk** - Incremental changes allow for more manageable risk as adjustments are made in controlled, small increments

- **Sustainability** - The architecture can endure and remain effective over the long term as it evolves in response to emerging challenges and opportunities

- **Continuous Improvement** - Regular updates and improvements based on ongoing feedback loops help ensure that the system remains aligned with business needs and technology trends

### Implementation

To implement an evolutionary architecture, we will adopt practices like continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD), which facilitate frequent updates. We will rely heavily on automation, such as automated contract testing, to maintain stability and ensure that changes do not break existing functionality. Our overall goal is to create a system that can continually evolve without requiring extensive rework or becoming obsolete, thereby supporting the long-term growth and changing needs of the organization.

Evolutionary architecture is particularly beneficial in environments like ours where business requirements change frequently or where innovation is critical to success. It will allow us to be more responsive and agile, hopefully turning the ability to adapt quickly into a competitive advantage.

# Engineering Career Path Framework

**DEPARTMENT: ENGINEERING**

*This career path framework is specific to the Engineering department and defines progression for technical roles including Software Engineering, Data Engineering, Infrastructure Engineering, and Machine Learning/Data Science.*

---

## Overview

### Why Have A Formally Defined Set of Career Paths?

Wiser is no longer a 6 person development team. We have grown over the last few years to be over 170+ people and will continue to grow in the coming years. Titles, roles and expectations have been different between teams. A formally defined career path will provide clarity and structure. It will align expectations between managers and their team members. It will create better review conversations and provide better direction for everyone on how they need to grow to achieve a promotion.

### Why Two Tracks?

We have two tracks: **Maker** and **Management**. Not everyone wants to manage people, it is a very different job from writing code. We do not want team members to feel like they are forced into managing people in order to progress. The Maker track provides for this continued advancement. The Maker track also has an additional branch for people who are interested to specialize in Architecture after they reach a certain level.

### Leadership Team

Within Engineering, we have our most senior team members work together as a team to help guide the department - what we do, how we do it, how we work together, etc. There are two levels. The leadership team includes both people on the Maker and the Manager Track, as we expect our most senior team members to contribute to the strategic leadership of the department. Team members above a certain level are nominated to join the LT or SLT by a current member of the SLT based on demonstrated strategic contribution and engagement within the Engineering organization. Nominations are reviewed by the SLT and approved by the CTO.

- **Engineering Senior Leadership Team (SLT)** - Eligible team members in all levels greater than and equal to L7
- **Engineering Leadership Team (LT)** - Eligible team members in all levels greater than and equal to L5

---

## Important Notes

- Each step along the path is defined in terms of the scope of contribution to Wiser
- The expectations at each level are consistent across engineering discipline (software engineering, infrastructure, data engineering, etc.)
- The expectations at each title are not currently consistent across departments within Wiser. The executive team will be working in the near future to determine how we might bring consistency across the whole company
- The expectations at each level may not be consistent with expectations when you were hired into the organization. Managers will address this with their team members on a case-by-case basis
- Levels and titles are based loosely on practices published by other tech companies. This does not mean that you should expect a strong equivalency if a level/title happens to line up with those at another company (don't assume an L7 Google role is equivalent to an L7 Wiser role). We have defined our career paths in a way that works best for our company
- We (will shortly) have full role descriptions for each level available for each engineering discipline - they are linked below. Not all disciplines will have all levels
- Each role description is a guideline and starting point for conversation with your manager, not a checklist. There is no formula for "do X things at the next level" to get promoted. When you are generally exceeding expectations at your current level and doing some of the critical things successfully at the next level, your manager will advocate for your promotion
- In some cases there may not be a need or there may not be availability for promotion to a certain level (silly example, but there can only be one CTO)
- Manager track team members can/will manage team members up to their current level (ex. an L7 people manager can manage another L7 manager or maker, but would not directly manage an L8 or above)

## Career Path Levels

### L1 - Learning

**Level Description:**  
Entry-level for bootcamp grads / people with no formal work experience. Focus is on learning & deepening fundamental CS skills rather than producing value. L1s deliver small features with frequent direct supervision and input from more senior team members.

**Titles:**
- Associate Software Engineer
- Associate Data Engineer
- Associate Infrastructure Engineer

---

### L2 - Recent Grad

**Level Description:**  
Entry-level for new CS grads. People join having good CS fundamentals. There is an expectation of learning at this level, but also of independent contribution of value. L2s should be able to independently solve well-scoped problems with less frequent input and direct supervision from more senior team members.

**Titles:**
- Software Engineer
- Data Engineer
- Infrastructure Engineer
- Machine Learning Engineer / Data Scientist

---

### L3 - Mid-Level

**Level Description:**  
Hires at this level typically have a few years of professional experience. Mastery of CS fundamentals and core engineering skills. Comfortable stewarding end-to-end features, has a deep understanding of the parts of the business related to their team, starts to mentor more junior engineers.

**Titles:**
- Software Engineer II
- Data Engineer II
- Infrastructure Engineer II
- Machine Learning Engineer II / Data Scientist II

---

### L4 - Senior

**Level Description:**  
Stewards large end-to-end initiatives, technical expert for other team members, assists with interviewing. Works directly with product team on solution discovery, feasibility analysis, forecasting and prioritization. Drives technical design and collaborates well with peers on other teams to drive improvements. Demonstrated technical leadership is important at this level.

**Titles:**
- Senior Software Engineer
- Senior Data Engineer
- Senior Infrastructure Engineer
- Senior Machine Learning Engineer / Senior Data Scientist

---

### L5 - Lead

**Level Description:**  
Clearly demonstrates technical breadth and depth. L5s are seen as a leader and contributors engaging across the entire Engineering department. Expert in the business domain of their product line, strong communicator, role model and driver of best practices. IC track L5s at this level demonstrate practice leadership. Manager L5s are splitting time between hands on contribution and people managing a team.

**Maker Track Titles:**
- Lead Software Engineer
- Lead Data Engineer
- Lead Infrastructure Engineer
- Lead Machine Learning Engineer / Lead Data Scientist

**Architect Track Titles:**
- Software Architect
- Data Architect
- Infrastructure Architect

**Manager Track Titles:**
- Engineering Manager

---

### L6 - Principal

**Level Description:**  
L6 team members have broad impact to the whole Engineering department. These team members have the knowledge, experience and sound judgement to be force multipliers on any project/team to which they are assigned and make the entire org better. L6s contribute to the overall technical vision and ensure we are achieving it. L6 managers may lead multiple teams, contribute to people and process best practices, and may not be directly responsible for hands-on contribution.

**Maker Track Titles:**
- Principal Software Engineer
- Principal Data Engineer
- Principal Infrastructure Engineer
- Principal Machine Learning Engineer / Principal Data Scientist

**Architect Track Titles:**
- Principal Software Architect
- Principal Data Architect
- Principal Infrastructure Architect

**Manager Track Titles:**
- Senior Engineering Manager

---

### L7 - Director

**Level Description:**  
L7s deliver value that materially change outcomes for the company. ICs are considered an authority and thought leader in multiple areas. They are the leaders that are inventing new transformational IP. L7 managers own and are the deep technical expert in a discipline or portfolio/product line. L7 managers drive continuous process improvement and are responsible for portfolio staffing, planning and budgeting. L7 managers are cross discipline proactive leaders that solve our most difficult business and technical problems.

**Maker Track Titles:**
- Staff Software Engineer
- Staff Data Engineer
- Staff Infrastructure Engineer
- Staff Machine Learning Engineer / Staff Data Scientist

**Architect Track Titles:**
- Staff Software Architect
- Staff Data Architect
- Staff Infrastructure Architect

**Manager Track Titles:**
- Director, Engineering

---

### L8 - Senior Director

**Level Description:**  
At this level, the primary IC focus is industry thought leadership and the management track focus is strategic thinking and execution. L8 ICs are outward facing evangelists that attract talent to the company. L8 managers identify, drive and successfully deliver on new material opportunities for the company. These leaders have a clearly articulated vision, high level roadmap and technical strategy for their discipline / product portfolio. Leaders are clearly recognized by other company leaders and execs as strategic partners.

**Maker Track Titles:**
- Staff Software Engineer
- Staff Data Engineer
- Staff Infrastructure Engineer
- Staff Machine Learning Engineer / Staff Data Scientist

**Manager Track Titles:**
- Senior Director, Engineering

---

### L9 - Vice President

**Level Description:**  
L9s are strategic leaders with a greater scope of responsibility than L8s, typically leading a product line w/multiple product portfolios or technology disciplines. They also demonstrate a broad impact across the company beyond their area of ownership. L9s develop other senior leaders, improve the leadership effectiveness of the engineering organization, and develop effective long term organization growth plans. These leaders should also be outward facing to prospects and customers when needed to close a deal or address an urgent need.

**Maker Track Titles:**
- Distinguished Software Engineer
- Distinguished Data Engineer
- Distinguished Infrastructure Engineer
- Distinguished Machine Learning Engineer / Distinguished Data Scientist

**Manager Track Titles:**
- Vice President, Engineering

---

### L10 - Senior VP / CTO

**Level Description:**  
The executive responsible for the technical foundation of the company. Broadly accountable for the performance of the entire development organization. Collaborates with other executives to set corporate strategic direction. Outward facing to the rest of the company, customers and the industry.

**Manager Track Titles:**
- Senior Vice President Engineering / Chief Technology Officer
